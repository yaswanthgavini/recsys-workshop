Step 7 — Top-N recommendations

def top_n(algo, uid, n=5, min_pop=20):
    seen = {raw item ids the user rated}
    cands = [i for i in all_items if i not in seen and n_ratings[i] >= min_pop]
    scored = sorted(((i, algo.predict(uid, i).est) for i in cands), key=lambda t: -t[1])[:n]

Step 8 — Experiment: how many latent factors?

   20       0.8791 ± 0.0012

  100       0.8828 ± 0.0009
  200       0.8882 ± 0.0017
Does more factors ⇒ better performance?

No. Flat, then rising: more capacity ⇒ more overfitting on ~130 ratings/user and ~8/movie. Differences of 0.001 are within seed noise → always repeat.

Step 9 — Prediction ≠ Top-N recommendation

                               Precision@10  Recall@10
popularity (non-personalised)        0.1173     0.0860
SVD (50 factors)                     0.0593     0.0384
SVD tuned                            0.0537     0.0381

Relevant = test rating ≥ 4 · candidates = movies with ≥ 20 training ratings · 300 users

The best RMSE model does not win the top-10 list! Popularity is a brutal baseline: (1) SVD optimises rating error, not ranking; (2) niche items can have inflated predictions; (3) “not in test” ≠ “disliked” (data is missing-not-at-random).

Try: candidates with ≥ 100 ratings, or blend SVD score with popularity.
