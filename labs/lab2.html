Step 2 — The training matrix in NumPy

R = np.full((n_users, n_items), np.nan)          # NaN = unknown
R[train.userId.map(u_idx), train.movieId.map(i_idx)] = train.rating.values
M    = ~np.isnan(R)                               # mask of known cells
mu_u = np.nanmean(R, axis=1)                      # each user's mean
Rc   = np.where(M, R - mu_u[:, None], 0.0)        # centred, unknown → 0
R: (610, 9724) | known cells: 80,668 | sparsity 98.64%
Only training ratings go in. The 20% test ratings stay hidden until evaluation.

Unknown → 0 after centring means “no opinion” (it is the user’s average) — not “hated it”.

Step 4 — Predict and recommend

def predict_ui(s, j, k=20):
    r = raters[j]                                   # users who rated movie j
    sr = s[r]
    if len(r) > k: top = np.argpartition(-sr, k-1)[:k]; r, sr = r[top], sr[top]   # k most similar raters
    pos = sr > 0; r, sr = r[pos], sr[pos]            # positive similarity only
    return (sr @ Rc[r, j]) / np.abs(sr).sum(), len(r)   # weighted avg of centred ratings
$$\hat r_{ui}=\bar r_u+\frac{\sum_{v\in N_k(u,i)} s_{uv}(r_{vi}-\bar r_v)}{\sum_{v\in N_k(u,i)}|s_{uv}|}$$

Nk(u,i) = the k most similar users who rated i. Then: rank unrated movies by prediction, drop the unreliable ones.

Rule: require evidence (support) before you recommend.

Why is k = 20 identical to k = 50?

Each candidate was rated by only 3–10 positively-similar users. If fewer than k neighbours rated a movie, a larger k changes nothing → sparsity, not k, limits the neighbourhood.
